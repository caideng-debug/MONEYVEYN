# VEYNHOLD: Story Bible

*Dark, grounded medieval fantasy. Parry combat. One sword, one rope that failed, one king who wasn't surprised.*

This document is canon for narrative, quests, lore items and dialogue. It agrees with `src/data/content.js` (writ, tally, hymn, muster roll, confession, Tamsin, Ilsabet, Odran's dying words). Where the two disagree, update this file, not the shipped text.

---

## 1. Premise and Tone

Wren Aldmoor, Ward of the Lantern Court, is hanged at dawn on the Long Gallows by order of King Aurec, the man who raised her. The stones under the gallows drink the oath from every dying subject of Veynhold. Wren never swore one, so the earth spits her back. She climbs out of a gravedigger's pit with her neck still bruised. To find out why the King hanged someone he knew would live, she cuts her way north toward the Crownlands.

**Tone rules**
- Magic is rare, physical and unpleasant. It looks like red stone, hollow wood, black water and ash. Nobody throws fireballs.
- Everyone has a reason. Nobody is evil for the fun of it, and the King least of all.
- Grief is shown in small acts: a name cut into a post, a lamp-flask handed over.
- The world is tired. People have lived under one king for 71 years and have stopped asking questions.

---

## 2. Kingdom History

**The Long Hunger (about 75 years ago).** Seven failed harvests. The old dynasty collapsed and the March farms emptied. People ate bark in Hollowwood and drowned themselves in the March.

**The Descent.** Aurec was a minor lord's younger son, Third of his Name only by an old line. He went down into the hill beneath the old hold carrying a lantern. Under it he found the **Vein**: a red seam running beneath the whole kingdom, alive in the way a root is alive. It hungers for one thing, the weight of a sworn word. Aurec swore to it (the **First Oath**) and climbed out. That spring the Crownlands bloomed.

**The Oath Order.** Aurec was crowned the Lantern King. He founded the Lantern Court, raised **oathstones** in every village, and gave the **Abbey of Ash** the job of writing and keeping the rite of swearing. At sixteen, every subject swears fealty at their oathstone, to King, to Abbey and to the land. Soldiers swear again when they muster. Officials swear on taking office. The harvests never failed again.

**What nobody says aloud.** When a sworn person dies on Veynhold soil, the Vein takes the oath back, and the stones beneath them turn red. It drinks the oath, and with it whatever the oath held of the person. *"Swear, and it keeps you. Die, and it keeps you still."* The more oaths it is fed, the richer the Crownlands grow. In recent decades it has needed more and more.

**Political structure**
- **The King**: Aurec, Third of the Name. He has ruled 71 years and still sits a horse like a young man.
- **The Lantern Court**: the King's household and government in the Crownlands. Stewards, ledger-keepers and Wards (children of the realm raised at court as royal servants; Wren was the last of them).
- **The Wardens**: the King's justice. The Iron Warden (Odran Vale, headsman for forty years) ran the Execution Grounds. Lesser wardens hold the March sluices and the forest roads.
- **The Abbey of Ash**: the clergy. It writes the oath-rite, keeps the swearing rolls, burns and blesses the dead. Its militant arm is the **Penitent order**, the cowled jailers who pray at the gallows.
- **The Marches and the Wood**: the Drowned March supplies the levies, and Hollowwood supplies timber. Both are poor. The Crownlands eat well.
- **The Ledgers**: the sealed oath-tithe ledgers kept in the Lantern Court. Each one records how many oaths every region "returned" each season. These are what Wren broke open (see the Writ of Execution).

## 3. King Aurec

**Reputation.** He saved the kingdom and has never been seen to age badly. To the Crownlands he is a living blessing. To the March he is the man who takes the levy. To Hollowwood he is the man whose huntsmen burn out anyone who refuses the oathstone. Nobody alive remembers another king.

**The truth.** Aurec cannot die. The First Oath bound him to feed the Vein, and the Vein keeps what it is sworn to. He has been dying slowly for decades and the Vein will not let him finish. He feels every oath it drinks. He is ninety-odd years old in a body that refuses to fail, and he is exhausted.

**Motives.** Two, and both are true.
1. **He wants to end.** A sworn man cannot break his own oath, and no sworn hand can harm the Vein's heart. The hymn says it plainly: *"Only the oathless walk out of the hill."*
2. **He wants the kingdom to keep eating.** If the Vein dies, the Long Hunger comes back. He believes an oathless keeper could hold the Vein without feeding it oaths, because the Vein cannot drink what was never sworn. It would gnaw on that keeper forever instead.

**Relationship to Wren.** He found her as an infant in a burned Unsworn camp in Hollowwood and gave orders that she must **never swear**. He raised her as his Ward, taught her the sword himself, and loved her. That is real, and it is also the problem. He raised her as a key. He left the ledger room unsealed so that her conscience would do the rest. He wrote "by the King's mercy" on her writ and watched the hanging to prove that the earth would refuse her. (The confession: *"He looked like a man who had been proven right."*) He then rode home to wait for her to come.

He will tell her all of it. He won't apologise for loving her or for using her, because to him those were never two separate things.

## 4. The Central Mystery

**"Why does a man hang someone he knows will live?"** (Odran's dying question.)

The mystery unfolds in layers:
1. Why didn't the earth take Wren? *(Answered in region 1: she never swore.)*
2. What does the Vein drink, and where do the bodies go? *(Hollowwood and the March.)*
3. Why was Wren never allowed to swear, and where did she come from? *(Hollowwood.)*
4. Why is the King still alive? *(Abbey of Ash.)*
5. What does he want from her? *(Crownlands.)*

---

## 5. Factions

**1. The Lantern Court (the Crown)**
*Wants:* to preserve the order, the harvests and the King. Most courtiers don't know the truth. The senior stewards do, and they fear a famine more than they fear sin.
*Face:* **Steward Maelis Corran**, keeper of the Ledgers, a dry and frightened bureaucrat.
*Tools:* Wardens, levies, oathstones, the Huntsmen.

**2. The Penitent Order**
*Wants:* the Vein fed. They worship it as the land's god and consider dying sworn a sacrament. To them Wren is an abomination, a body the earth vomited up. They'd like to correct that by swearing her at sword-point and hanging her properly.
*Face:* **Brother Sallow, the Smiling Penitent.**
*Tools:* jailers, gallows chapels, the Abbey's lower cloisters.

**3. The Abbey of Ash (the Abbess's faction)**
*Wants:* absolution. They wrote the rite, so they know exactly what an oath weighs. The current Abbess has spent years quietly gathering proof, and she sent the novice Ilke to Wren. She wants the rite unwritten, but she fears what happens to the faithful when the Vein dies.
*Face:* **Abbess Hester Vane**, Odran's younger sister, which is why he sent Wren to her.
*Tools:* the swearing rolls (a name for every oath), the ash-rites, scholarship.

**4. The Unsworn (and the Cranes)**
*Wants:* to be left alone, and more and more often, the Vein destroyed. They are people of Hollowwood who refused the oathstone generation after generation, joined now by deserters, escaped levies and Ilsabet's surviving Cranes. They make the oil for the Vigil Lanterns.
*Face:* **Mother Rook**, a blind elder, and **Ilsabet Crane** once she is freed.
*Tools:* the deep wood, lantern-craft, and long memories.

## 6. Key NPCs

**Wren Aldmoor (player).** About twenty years old. Disciplined, court-trained, unsworn. She broke the ledgers because she counted the numbers. She is not a rebel by temperament. Her arc runs from obedient ward, to a woman who learns she was made for a purpose, to the person who decides what that purpose means. She speaks little. Her dialogue choices set the tone.

**King Aurec.** See §3. Arc: he waits, and in the end he either rests, rules on through Wren, or makes her his heir.

**Tamsin Ferrow, gravedigger.** Thirty years burying the hanged without looking at their faces. If the Names quest is finished, she learns she buried her own son, Jory. *Arc:* numbness, then grief, then purpose. If the names were returned, she later walks to the Abbey with the post's names to add them to the Abbess's unwriting (this enables the Names ending). If they weren't, she stays at the pit and the post stays blank.

**Ilsabet Crane, Captain of the Cranes.** Sardonic and fearless, and she hasn't forgiven herself for the levies she couldn't turn around in time. *Arc:* if freed, she becomes Wren's ally in Hollowwood, leads the Unsworn's fighters in the March, and stands at the hill's mouth in the finale. If refused, she dies in the gibbet. Wren later finds her feather on a Hollowwood Huntsman as a trophy, and the Unsworn are slow to trust her.

**Brother Sallow, the Smiling Penitent.** He smiles because he truly believes every hanging is mercy. He was **Ilke's confessor** and reported her for carrying letters, so his testimony hanged her. *Arc:* in the Execution Grounds chapel he duels Wren (optional), loses, and withdraws ("Penitents don't die unforgiven"). At the Abbey he leads the Penitent coup against the Abbess. If Wren tells him Ilke's name is cut into Tamsin's post (Names quest complete), his smile breaks. He can be **spared and turned**, and he will hold the Abbey door for Hester. Otherwise he must be killed.

**Odran Vale, the Iron Warden (dead after region 1).** He tied every knot himself for forty years and knew what the stones did. He wrote the confession. He is present after death through his key, his yoke and his sister.

**Gaol Sergeant Brask.** "B." of the Gaoler's Tally. He wrote down what Odran told him not to. Even though Brask dies in the fight, his tally is the first proof that the earth refused Wren.

**Abbess Hester Vane.** Precise, ashamed and brave in a slow way. She can die in the Penitent coup if Wren is too slow or sides with Sallow.

**Mother Rook.** The Unsworn elder in Hollowwood. She remembers the raid that took an infant, and she knows Wren by a birthmark-scar on her palm, the Unsworn "lamp-brand" given to newborns.

**Garrick Thorne, the King's Huntsman (Hollowwood boss).** His hooks weapon becomes the Huntsman's Hooks. He burned Wren's birth-camp twenty years ago on the King's order to "bring the child, kill the rest." He is not sorry.

**Sir Edric Hollin, Tide Warden (Drowned March boss).** Keeper of the Saltmere sluices, carries the Tide Halberd. He marched the ninth levy into the water and hanged the survivors to "complete the tithe." He drinks so he doesn't have to remember.

**Steward Maelis Corran.** Keeper of the Ledgers in the Crownlands. She can be persuaded to surrender the original ledgers, or she flees with them.

## 7. The Five Regions

The Vein runs beneath all five. Its red is visible in gallows stones, root-hollows, flooded oathstones, ash-kilns and finally the hill itself. The route runs north and inward: **Execution Grounds → Hollowwood → Drowned March → Abbey of Ash → Crownlands.**

**1. The Execution Grounds.** The Long Gallows, the Gaol, the Muster Field and the Headsman's Stair. It was built where the Vein runs close beneath the surface: the old kings learned that the dead here "rested easy." Penitents pray here and gaolers strip the name-tags. *Connects:* north through the Headsman's Gate (Headsman's Key) onto the Hollowwood road.

**2. Hollowwood.** An ancient forest whose trees are hollow all the way down because the Vein drinks from their roots. It was the refuge of the Unsworn for generations, and the Huntsmen burn it a camp at a time. Heartwood that survives is rich in sap (Heartwood Sap). *Connects:* the hollow root-channels drain south-east into the March, and a flooded root-tunnel is the shortcut.

**3. The Drowned March.** Former farmland that sank as the Vein drained it. The levies are drawn from here. Saltmere is a flooded crossing where the ninth levy (212 sworn) marched into the water against no enemy. Drowned oathstones show red under the shallows. A sunken bell tower gives the Drowned Bell. *Connects:* the causeway rises onto the Abbey's ash-hill, and the sluice gates control the water level (see Key Choices).

**4. The Abbey of Ash.** It sits on a hill of ash from seventy years of burning the dead. Below it are the kilns and the archive of swearing rolls. The Penitents run the lower cloisters and the Abbess holds the upper library. The Rite of Ash and the Ash Censer are found here. *Connects:* the old pilgrim road and a hidden kiln-tunnel lead into the Crownlands. The tunnel follows the Vein.

**5. The Crownlands.** Impossibly green fields, fat cattle, the Lantern Court and the hold on the hill. **Lanternfall**, the blade Aurec carried into the hill, hangs over the empty throne, because the King now waits below, at the Vein's heart. *Connects:* down. Only the oathless can walk the final descent. Sworn companions stop at the threshold.

## 8. Revelations and Their Clues

Each revelation is foreshadowed in earlier regions before it is confirmed.

**R1. The Vein drinks the oaths of the dying.** *(Confirmed: Execution Grounds, Odran.)*
- Execution Grounds: red gallows stones; Tamsin's "red, then black"; the Gaoler's Tally ("Oaths returned: 14 ... —"); the Penitent's Hymn.

**R2. The levies are drowned on purpose to feed the Vein.** *(Confirmed: Drowned March, Saltmere.)*
- Execution Grounds: the Muster Roll ("Marched: 212. Returned: 0"); Ilsabet ("There was no enemy across the water"); **Jory's name-tag** is in the Grounds, yet he supposedly drowned "with no body." He survived Saltmere and was hanged.
- Hollowwood: Cranes' tally-sticks with marks for drowned levies; an Unsworn tells how levies "go to the water singing their oaths."
- Drowned March: Hollin's sluice log ("water raised before muster, as instructed"); a gibbet row of Saltmere survivors at the Tide Warden's tower.

**R3. Wren was born Unsworn and taken from Hollowwood.** *(Confirmed: Hollowwood, Mother Rook.)*
- Execution Grounds: the writ ("none was sworn"); Ilsabet ("Odd thing, for the King's own shadow"); the hymn's "oathless."
- Hollowwood: Vigil Lanterns burn the same oil as Unsworn camp lamps, and the lamp "keeps" Wren; a burned camp holds a child's cradle with the lamp-brand carved on it, matching the scar on Wren's palm; Thorne: "You've grown. Last time you fit in my glove."

**R4. Aurec cannot die. The First Oath binds him to the Vein.** *(Confirmed: Abbey of Ash, the First Roll.)*
- Execution Grounds: "Seventy-one years, and he still sits a horse like a young man"; "Die, and it keeps you still."
- Hollowwood: an Unsworn song of "the lantern man who went into the hill and never came all the way out."
- Drowned March: a drowned shrine fresco shows a young Aurec with a lantern, and his face is identical to the King's face on recent coins.
- Abbey: the First Roll has a single oath and no death date, and the ink is still wet-red.

**R5. Aurec engineered Wren's crime and her hanging.** *(Confirmed: Crownlands, the Ledger Room and Aurec.)*
- Execution Grounds: "By the King's mercy"; Odran: "He looked like a man who had been proven right."
- Abbey: Hester reveals that Ilke's letters to Wren were intercepted *and then allowed through*. The intercepting seal is the King's, not the Penitents'.
- Crownlands: the ledger room's lock was never forced. Steward Corran's order book reads "Leave the east ledger room unwarded, by royal instruction," dated a week before Wren's arrest.

**R6. Aurec wants an oathless keeper, not just his own death.** *(Confirmed: the Vein's heart.)*
- Abbey: Hester's research: "The Vein cannot drink what was not sworn. It would have to hold it."
- Crownlands: Lanternfall's hilt is wrapped in the same red thread as a cradle-blanket; the Wards' quarters are empty except for Wren's room, which is kept exactly as she left it; Aurec's note on the empty throne: "Come down, Wren. I'll explain everything. I owe you that."

## 9. Story Structure

### Beginning: *The Rope That Broke* (Execution Grounds)
Wren wakes in Tamsin's pit, recovers her blade, kindles the first Vigil Lantern and fights through the Gaol and the Muster Field. Optional: free Ilsabet, recover the three names, and face Sallow in the gallows chapel. She defeats Odran Vale. His dying words reframe the game around one question, and the Headsman's Key opens the Hollowwood Gate.

### Middle: *What the Earth Keeps* (Hollowwood → Drowned March → Abbey of Ash)
- **Hollowwood:** Wren finds the Unsworn, learns her origin (R3) and kills Garrick Thorne. Ilsabet, if alive, joins as a recurring ally.
- **Drowned March:** at Saltmere she learns the levies are drowned deliberately (R2) and defeats Sir Edric Hollin. She faces the **Sluice Choice**.
- **Abbey of Ash:** the Penitent coup is underway. Wren reaches Abbess Hester, reads the First Roll (R4) and confronts Brother Sallow. Hester explains what an oath weighs and what an oathless hand can do.

### End: *Lanternfall* (Crownlands)
Wren walks into a kingdom that is too green. In the Lantern Court she finds proof she was set up (R5) and takes Lanternfall from the throne. Then she descends alone into the hill. Aurec waits at the Vein's heart, a red seam pulsing like a throat. He tells her everything (R6) and makes his offer. Depending on her choices, the scene goes to a duel, a surrender, or an unwriting.

**Final duel: King Aurec.** He fights in three phases. Phase 1 is a court-trained swordsman, the same teacher who taught Wren and reads her tells. Phase 2 draws on the Vein, which gives him perilous red strikes and posture that refuses to break. Phase 3 is a tired old man fighting honestly, slower. If Wren lands the deathblow with an oathless hand, the Vein lets him go.

## 11. Key Choices

1. **Ilsabet's cage** (free, leave or refuse): `flag:crane_freed` / `flag:crane_refused`.
2. **Names for the Dead** (complete or not): `q:names==4`.
3. **Mother Rook's request** (Hollowwood): show the Unsworn the hidden route to the March, which saves them but exposes them to the Crown, or keep it secret. Shown: Unsworn fighters appear in the March and the Crownlands. Kept: they survive the winter whatever happens.
4. **The Sluice Choice** (Drowned March): **open** the Saltmere sluices, which drains the crossing, uncovers the drowned levy for burial and floods two March villages; or **hold** them, which leaves the dead under water and spares the villages. Open is needed for the full Names ending, and it costs the March's goodwill.
5. **The Abbey coup**: side with Hester or with Sallow, then spare or kill Sallow.
6. **The final offer** at the Vein's heart: **strike** (duel), **take the Vein**, **swear**, or, if the conditions are met, **unwrite**.

## 12. Endings

### Main Ending: *The Lean Winter*
**Condition:** at the final offer, Wren chooses to strike and defeats Aurec. This is the default whenever the Names conditions aren't met.
Her oathless blade cuts the Vein's heart. Aurec dies at last, gratefully, with his hand on her face: "There. That's all I wanted." The stones across Veynhold go grey. The Crownlands' green fades within a season and winter comes hungry. No one swears at an oathstone again. The epilogue cards depend on earlier choices: the March, Hollowwood, the Abbey (Hester or the Penitents), and Tamsin's post. The final card shows Wren in the empty Lantern Court handing out the royal granary.

### Alternate Ending A: *The Lantern Keeper*
**Condition:** at the final offer, choose **take the Vein**. Always available.
Wren lays her oathless palm on the heart and the Vein, unable to drink her, holds onto her instead. Aurec crumbles to dust, finally free. The harvests continue and no more oaths are needed. Wren stays in the hill forever while it gnaws on her, never dying and never leaving. Epilogue: decades on, a girl in the Crownlands asks why the lantern in the hill never goes out. **Variant:** if Ilsabet is alive, she comes down to the threshold every year to talk to her.

### Alternate Ending B: *The King's Mercy*
**Condition:** at the final offer, choose **swear**. Available only if at least two of these are true: `crane_refused`, Sallow killed, Abbess Hester dead, Names incomplete.
Wren kneels and swears to Aurec as his heir. The earth can keep her now. Aurec gives her the First Oath's burden and dies. Queen Wren rules as he did: the levies march, the stones go red, and the harvests are perfect. The last shot is a new Ward, a child, being told never to swear.

### Alternate Ending C (best): *Names for the Dead*
**Condition:** at the final offer, choose **unwrite**. Requires all of these:
- `q:names==4`
- `flag:crane_freed`
- Hester alive and allied
- Sallow spared and turned
- Saltmere sluices opened
- The original ledgers delivered to the Abbey

Wren calls the others down to the threshold. Hester reads the swearing rolls and the ledgers aloud. Tamsin reads the post. Sallow reads Ilke's name. Each named oath goes back to the dead it was taken from. The Vein grows weaker with every name read. Aurec puts down his sword and asks Wren to read his name last. There is no duel. The Vein lets everyone go at once, the King included. The abundance fades over three years instead of one season, and Unsworn lantern-craft and the opened granaries carry the kingdom through. Epilogue: Tamsin's post, covered in names, stands where the Long Gallows used to be.

## 13. Dialogue Voice Notes

**General:** plain words and short sentences. No modern slang and no "thee" or "thou." People talk about the work in front of them: knots, graves, water, ledgers. Gods are named in oaths ("oh, gods") but nobody prays with much conviction. The kingdom's taboo is talking about the stones, so characters circle around it.

- **Wren (choices):** brief, direct and court-polite even when angry. She gives no speeches. Her best lines are questions. *"Why didn't it take me?"*
- **Aurec:** warm, patient and grandfatherly, with a terrible calm underneath. He says "child" and "Wren," never "Ward." He reasons rather than commands. *"I never lied to you. I simply waited until you could bear it."*
- **Tamsin:** working-class, rambling when nervous and blunt when tired. She repeats herself. Earth and digging come into everything she says. *"Nobody climbs out of my pit."*
- **Ilsabet:** dry, mocking and soldierly. She jokes in the face of death and goes quiet and exact when talking about the levy. *"Either the rope was rotten or you're a ghost with good boots."*
- **Brother Sallow:** gentle, smiling and pastoral. He uses "mercy," "rest" and "kept" the way other people use "kill." He never raises his voice. *"Hush. The earth only wants what you owe it."* Once broken, he speaks in fragments.
- **Odran Vale (posthumous, through texts):** craftsman's pride and terse confession. *"My knots hold."*
- **Abbess Hester Vane:** scholarly and precise. She speaks in weights and measures, and she is ashamed in footnotes. *"An oath weighs exactly as much as the person who swore it."*
- **Mother Rook:** old, slow and sung half the time. She speaks of the lantern and the hill as if they were people. *"You came back burning, little lamp."*
- **Garrick Thorne:** casual cruelty and hunting metaphors. *"Easy now. You always did bolt."*
- **Sir Edric Hollin:** drunk, self-pitying and defensive. He hides behind orders. *"I raised the water. I didn't make it hungry."*
- **Steward Corran:** clipped bureaucratic phrasing that cracks under fear. *"Per royal instruction. It was always per royal instruction."*
